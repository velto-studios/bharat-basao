## 3. Observed visual identity

- **Colour:** saffron/orange accent, deep navy, ocean blue and green terrain. Orange flag badge with "DAY N" on every cover; Hindi counter "जनसंख्या" top-right.
- **Typography:** heavy white Devanagari headline ("37 नई जगह") with an orange underline, Hinglish subline. Rounded geometric sans on the site.
- **Illustration:** generic low-poly 3D (stock-looking temples, trees, cars) on a stylised relief map. No characters, no faces, no mascot.
- **Motion:** slow camera pans and zooms over the map, pins popping in, simple text pops. Ambient music (Pixabay credited on Day 2).
- **Covers:** strong and consistent grid: same badge, same layout, a single 3D object per cover.

## 4. Strongest creative patterns

1. **Participation as the product.** "Follow + comment your city = a house with your name." The viewer is literally in the next episode.
2. **A visible counter.** Population 0 → 9 → 505 → 978 → 2,977 → 4,207. Progress is the story.
3. **Daily episodes with a promised tomorrow** ("Kal 25 nayi jagah"). Creates a habit and a reason to follow.
4. **Public crediting by handle** in the caption and story. People share because their name is in it.
5. **A real website that matches the Reel.** "Find your house" search turns a Reel into a destination.
6. **Festival tie-in** (Diwali night, diyas, fireworks) for an emotional beat.

## 5. Storytelling techniques

- Hook = a number or an absence ("Population 0", "Na sadak", "1505 comments!").
- Payoff = the viewer's own contribution made visible.
- Rule-setting in the caption gives the audience a game to play (most-liked comment wins).
- Short, rhythmic Hinglish lines ("Na sadak. Na ghar. Na bijli.").

## 6. Weaknesses and missed opportunities

- **No character, no emotion on screen.** Everything is objects and maps; nothing looks back at the viewer.
- **Generic 3D assets.** Stock-looking low-poly; no ownable art style. Covers are recognisable by layout, not by art.
- **Text-only storytelling.** No voiceover personality; the Reels rely on captions and music.
- **Sensitive framing, handled bluntly.** The undivided map (Karachi, Quetta, Yangon, Colombo appear on the site) draws heated comments, and the account answers with small disclaimers only. Velto keeps the Akhand Bharat premise (approved direction) but states community rules up front: respectful comments only, nothing against any country or community, and the map is presented as an imagined game map, not an official boundary.
- **Comment spam and moderation burden** (the account had to ask people to stop repeating comments).
- **Heavy dependence on one-off landmark lists**; after the novelty, the episodes look alike.
- **Disclaimers ("Sirf ek game hai. Imagined builds")** show the team is aware of the sensitivity.

## 7. What can be adapted at a conceptual level (not copied)

- A world that grows because the audience asks for it.
- A visible, honest counter.
- Daily or regular episodes with a "next time" promise.
- Crediting participants by name.
- A website where people find themselves.

We do not take the name, map, logo, 3D assets, captions, scripts, music or layouts.

## 9. Approved creative direction: Bharat Basao

**Bharat Basao: "Akhand Bharat. Ghar ghar se."** An illustrated, paper-cut map of the undivided subcontinent that followers settle together.

- **Start:** Din 1, Jansankhya 0. The first and only build is the Zero Mile Chaupal at Nagpur, the centre of the map.
- **Mascot:** Chiku Mistri, the sparrow builder who builds whatever the community asks for.
- **Explore:** search or tap any of 98 cities, zoom in, and open that city's gali.
- **Named houses:** visitors design a house that appears in their city's gali on their own device. It joins the public map in the next episode when they follow @velto_studios and comment their city and name (followers only, one comment each).
- **Community landmarks:** the most requested place on the Nirmaan board is built next.
- **Day/night:** every gali can switch to evening, with lit windows and lamps.
- **Series:** each Reel is one Din. The counter only ever shows real, published houses.

The participation loop that makes the reference work stays. What changes is the craft: an ownable character, a consistent illustrated world, a street-level view where people find their own name, and honest numbers.

_The earlier "Chirpur" sparrow-town proposal was rejected in favour of this direction and is not being pursued._
